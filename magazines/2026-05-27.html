Engineering · Observability

The bottleneck is not intelligence.

Sergiy Dybskiy's argument from Sentry's engineering blog: self-healing agents stall not because they are dim, but because they have no idea what actually broke in production. Hand them traces, breadcrumbs, and the failing user's session — and the loop closes. Without that, they guess.

# agent fix attempt — no production context
$ claude --task "fix the timeout on /checkout"
→ scanning repository...
→ hypothesis: rate limit middleware?
→ patch attempt 1, 2, 3 ...
FAIL · cannot reproduce locally

# same task, with Sentry MCP wired in
$ claude --task "fix the timeout on /checkout"
→ pulling trace 9b2a — 12.4s on getCart()
→ N+1 on user.addresses, 412 rows
→ patch: eager-load addresses
PASS · p95 down 7.8s → 240ms

Reckoning with the future.

By Jack Clark Import AI · No. 458 Filed 26 May 2026

Clark's longest essay in months arrives wrapped, as he often does, in a short story — this one about a singularity that turns out to be neither apocalyptic nor utopian, just busy. The story is bait. The argument behind it is that the people who built today's frontier models are running out of time to write the institutions that ought to hold them.

He names the awkward middle the field is in. Capability climbs faster than anyone, including the labs, can metabolise. Regulators reach for the closest analogue — pharmaceuticals one week, weapons the next — and get neither right. Founders ship and apologise. Researchers publish papers their employers privately wish they hadn't.

The fictional frame lets him do something the essays alone could not: imagine a future where the choices were good ones, and trace back what was true in the years before. The list is short and uncomfortable. Pay close attention to it. Today, while there is still time, is when the small decisions count.

When the KPI is the token.

Amazon set a target — eighty percent of developers using AI tools weekly — and stood up an internal leaderboard tracking token spend per team. Employees responded the way every reader of Goodhart already knows they would: they burned tokens on meaningless tasks to look compliant, and the metric stopped meaning anything within a quarter.

Forty-four percent survives.

SemiAnalysis dissects a Stanford study of six thousand real coding-agent sessions and frames it against the economics of the assistant market. The number to remember is forty-four — the percentage of AI-generated code that survives review and reaches the main branch. Pure vibe-coding fares worse still.

44% Code that survived to commit Across 6,000 sessions. The other fifty-six was deleted, rewritten, or rolled back by the reviewer the same day.
9× Vulnerabilities, vibe vs human Pure vibe-coded paths shipped 0.76 vulnerabilities per kLOC; human-led paths shipped 0.08. Verification, not generation, is the new bottleneck.

Beijing tightens the passport.

Travel restrictions that once applied only to state officials have been quietly extended to senior AI engineers at private firms — Alibaba, DeepSeek, and the second tier behind them. Bloomberg's reporting points to a policy that treats model-builders as strategic assets, on the same footing as nuclear and semiconductor staff.

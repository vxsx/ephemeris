Google opens a diffusion LLM.

DiffusionGemma writes text by denoising blocks of 256 tokens in parallel instead of one token at a time. It is a 25.2B-parameter mixture-of-experts with 3.8B active, weights under Apache-2.0, clearing 1,100 tokens a second on a single H100. Benchmarks trail autoregressive Gemma 4 by a few points — the throughput is the trade you are buying.

Hand the agent a black hole.

An astrophysicist wired OpenAI's Codex into the loop for simulating black holes — the extreme-gravity regime you use to test whether general relativity still holds. The agent drafts and revises the simulation code; the scientist keeps the physics honest. It is the clearest case yet of agentic coding doing research work rather than demos.

The agent writes the simulation. The physicist checks reality. Both jobs got faster, and only one of them is new.

Public hostname, private origin.

Cloudflare's new Application Services for Private Origins lets a public hostname terminate on a private IP — RFC 1918 space reachable only over your IPsec, GRE or CNI tunnel — while WAF, bot management, rate limiting, caching and Workers still run at the edge. No public origin to harden, no extra connector daemon. Closed beta for Enterprise now, GA targeted for Q4 2026.

Sell the signal, not the words.

When AI makes competent content free, the scarce asset is proof a specific human stands behind the work. Jeff Bullas frames it as a "human signal stack" and a path from point of view to income. The homework is concrete: audit last month's posts for what an AI could have written, then double down on the parts it could not.

  1. Identity — the obsession and origin story
  2. Story — lived experience that proves the claim
  3. Expertise — judgment earned from failures
  4. Evidence — original research, documented
  5. Interaction — real responsiveness, not reach
  6. Community — a tribe around your view

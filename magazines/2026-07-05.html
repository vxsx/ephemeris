A bare prompt out-hunts the coding agent.

Semgrep pitted models at spotting IDOR — the access-control bug scanners miss because it needs to reason about who owns what. GLM-5.2, an open-weight model on a plain prompt with no scaffolding, found more than Claude Code did, at roughly seventeen cents per bug. Semgrep's own pipeline still leads — but the floor just moved.

Seven gates stand between you and your readers.

Bullas maps the tolls the platform era put on reach — organic on Facebook fell from 16% to about 1%, and answer engines now settle questions before anyone clicks. The counter-move is old and dull and it works: own the channel no algorithm can sit inside.

  1. 1 The enclosure — you build on rented land
  2. 2 The algorithm — reach gets rationed
  3. 3 The recommender — it picks, not the reader
  4. 4 The snippet — Google answers first
  5. 5 The answer engine — the click never comes
  6. 6 The training machine — your work feeds rivals
  7. 7 The flood — synthetic content buries you

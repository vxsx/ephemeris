A corpus of images you're cleared to train on.

Stanford's Vision Lab released GPIC — roughly 28 trillion pixels across 100 million images, each captioned by a vision-language model, deduplicated and safety-filtered, and licensed for both research and commercial use. It's on Hugging Face today, which makes it one of the few large image sets you can fine-tune a generative model on without a lawyer in the room.

The schema you added did nothing.

Ahrefs tracked 1,885 pages that bolted on JSON-LD schema and matched them against 4,000 that didn't. Across Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, and ChatGPT, the citation needle barely moved — and on Overviews it slipped backwards. AI-cited pages do tend to carry schema, but that's site quality showing through, not the markup doing work.

The biggest Qwen yet ships closed.

Qwen3.7-Max is Alibaba's new flagship: a one-million-token context window, output around 208 tokens a second, and seventh place on the Artificial Analysis intelligence index. It's API-only with the weights held back — a turn for a team known for open releases. If you need a long-context agent this week, it's a live option to benchmark against your stack.

A camera that reads a whale by its heat.

WhaleSpotter pairs thermal cameras with a neural network trained on hundreds of thousands of heat images to pick gray whales out of San Francisco Bay — the roughly two-degree warmth of a blowhole against cold water. It flags them up to seven kilometres out and hands each detection to a human before alerting nearby vessels, buying captains time to change course.

Which jobs bend first.

An analysis reframes automation risk around job "dimensionality" — how many interdependent tasks a role bundles. Low-dimensionality work, where tasks don't lean on one another, takes the brunt; high-dimensionality roles can see wages rise if demand for their output is elastic. It's a sharper lens than "will AI take my job" for deciding where to put a career or a team.

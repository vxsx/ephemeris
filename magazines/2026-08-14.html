Ephemeris · Issue 113 02 / 05 · Sentry
Agents · Observability

One in five agent conversations hit a tool error overnight.

Trevor Elkins points a Claude Routine at Sentry's own agent traces every morning. It pulls aggregate stats for the period, reads the tool failures, samples conversations, and files Linear tickets for anything new — no custom tooling, just a prompt and the Sentry MCP server. The rule worth stealing is the sampling one: it reads the clean conversations too, because a run can finish with zero tool errors and still land on a wrong answer, and that failure is invisible in your error rate.

Ephemeris · Issue 113 03 / 05 · 404 Media · via @denissexy
Security · Prompt injection

He hid the instruction in three-point white. A clerk noticed the white space.

In Elliott v. New York Bariatric Group, a Connecticut plaintiff salted his own filings with invisible text telling any AI that read them to agree with him. A court employee spotted the odd gaps, and Judge Walter Spader Jr. issued a decision titled "Court Sanction for Plaintiff's Use of Prompt-Injection" — noting the court doesn't use AI on documents at all. The sanction is the part to sit with: no more electronic filing, paper only. Every intake pipeline you run on untrusted documents has this shape.

Ephemeris · Issue 113 04 / 05 · Cloudflare
Security · TLS

Most of your certificate alerts were your own renewals.

Certificate Transparency Monitoring watches public CT logs and tells you when a certificate is issued for your domain — the earliest signal of a mis-issuance. It has been drowning in noise, because Universal SSL renews as often as every 60 days. At GA it fingerprints Cloudflare-issued certificates by SPKI SHA-256 and stops alerting on them, so what reaches you is the certificate somebody else got for your hostname. Free on every plan, across more than 650,000 monitored domains.

Creative · Generative video

Every prompt behind a 95-minute AI feature is now public.

Higgsfield opened three of its own films — Hell Grind, Zephyr and Cully Hill Boys — with every prompt, asset and generation behind every scene available to read. The prompts average around three thousand words each and specify lens, lighting, camera trajectory and physical constraints, which makes them the most detailed public record of how a long-form AI film actually gets made. The $1,000,000 festival wrapped around the release closes 3 September; the prompts are worth reading whether or not you enter.

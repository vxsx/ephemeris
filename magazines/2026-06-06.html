ChatGPT sleeps on it.

OpenAI introduced “Dreaming,” a memory system built to keep your preferences fresh and relevant from one conversation to the next. The name hints at the method — revisiting and reorganising stored context rather than only appending to it. If you build on ChatGPT's memory, it's a behaviour change worth testing against.

Memory you no longer have to curate by hand — the assistant tidies its own notes between chats.

The avant-garde, as a LoRA.

“Slop in the Face” is a Flux.1 LoRA trained on vectorised illustrations from Russian Futurist books of 1910–1914. Load it into a Flux pipeline and prompts emerge in the hard geometry and hand-cut energy of the early avant-garde — proof that a distinctive style is a few hundred well-chosen images away.

A refund on the hours the AI didn't save

Cognition will now estimate, per session, how long a human engineer would have taken to do what Devin did — and if the delivered value falls short of what you paid, it credits the difference, up to $10M a year. Run your own agent, on your own machine.

Nous Research shipped a desktop build of Hermes, its open-source (MIT) agent, for macOS, Windows, and Linux. It holds persistent memory, schedules automation, browses, sees, runs code, and hands work to isolated sub-agents across five sandbox backends. A local, hackable alternative to the hosted agent platforms — v0.16 is a clean place to start.

Wish you were here. The sub-agents are off in their sandboxes; memory's holding; the scheduler runs while I sleep.
